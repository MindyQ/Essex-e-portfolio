Title: Security Risk Assessment – [Electronic Health Record]

1. Executive Summary (approx. 100 words)

Briefly describe what the system is, and what this report will cover.

An electronic health record system record and stores patients’ medical information in digital formThis report evaluates the potential security risks in EHR （electronic health record system） in hospitals. It brings benefits to both patients and hospitals, it saves repetitive health checking work. With comprehensive information, the health record systems increase the accuracy of diagnose. However, it also consists of key threats such data breaches, unauthorised access and misuses. This report will discuss its structure, its key threats and practical steps to reduce those risks.

2. System/Scenario Overview (approx. 100 words)

The EHR system records a patient's health data whenever they visit a hospital. The medical workers record the results of examination and diagnose in to the EHR. The data is shared among different departments of the hospitals and even among hospitals in the city. Medication insurance institutes also use the data to verify the reimbursement claims. Therefore, a patient's record travels well beyond one clinic. Doctors from different hospitals can access the history to support their own diagnosis. However, this wide sharing is also the source of risk. The more organisations hold the data, the larger the attack surface becomes.

3. Identified Threats & Vulnerabilities (approx. 200 words)

The system faces several threats. Firstly, excessive access is a major problem. The EHR connects to many third-party interfaces, including insurance bodies, testing laboratories, and external medical institutions. Each interface adds a new entry point for attackers.

Secondly, insider threats are hard to ignore. Medical workers with too much access may misuse or leak information. For example, some could sell patient details to pharmaceutical companies for commercial gain. Others might share records with unauthorised people.

Thirdly, data breaches remain a constant danger. Health information can be stolen if the system has security vulnerabilities. In 2021, Ireland's Health Service Executive (HSE) was attacked by hackers. The incident exposed the records of millions of patients and disrupted hospital services for weeks.

Finally, phishing attacks target the human layer. Medical workers may reveal login credentials through deceptive emails. Once an account is compromised, the attacker gains a legitimate-looking doorway into the system.

4. Recommendations (approx. 100–150 words)

The risks above can be reduced through several measures. Firstly, multi-factor authentication (MFA) should be enforced for every system login. This makes stolen passwords far less useful to attackers. Secondly, staff should receive regular training on recognising phishing emails. They also need clearer awareness of their legal duties when handling patient data. Thirdly, user roles must be defined precisely to prevent excessive access. The principle of least privilege should guide every permission setting. Besides, the system should encrypt data both in storage and during transfer. Constant maintenance is also necessary to close security vulnerabilities before they are exploited. Therefore, a combination of technology, training, and clear policy offers the strongest protection.

5. Conclusion (approx. 50–75 words)

Brief summary of key points and why this matters for the organisation.

The EHR system improves hospital efficiency and diagnosis accuracy, but it also brings the security risk. The main dangers are excessive access, insider misuse, data breaches, and phishing. Therefore, hospitals must treat security as a continuous task. Strong authentication, clear access rules, and staff training are essential to protect patient data.
