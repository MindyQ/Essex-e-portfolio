# Unit 10 · Strategic and Enterprise IT Management: Aligning IT with Business Goals

> How established IT governance frameworks (COBIT and ITIL) help organisations align technology with business strategy, shown through a Toyota case study and an ITIL service-management analysis.

## Case Study: Toyota and IT Governance Frameworks (COBIT & ITIL)

### How did Toyota leverage IT governance frameworks like COBIT and ITIL to align IT with its business objectives?
Toyota uses IT governance frameworks such as COBIT and ITIL to align its IT infrastructure with its business goals.

COBIT defines what IT needs to achieve to meet business targets. It sets high-level control requirements to manage risks, ensure regulatory compliance, and optimise resources; for example, it links IT investments to sustainability objectives, such as reducing carbon emissions through supply chain optimization. It helps organisations govern IT properly and set performance metrics to check whether IT delivers business value.

Toyota uses ITIL guidelines to deliver customer-focused services that deliver measurable value. Toyota implements cloud-based solutions for real-time monitoring and analysis of production data. This ensures real value is created and high-quality service is delivered. ITIL helps organisations run IT services efficiently and reliably in daily operations across its global supply chain.

### What role did digital transformation play in improving Toyota's global operations?
First, digital transformation streamlined IT services and automated supply chain processes. This removed manual work and reduced operational inefficiencies across its global supply network.

Second, with data-driven decision-making across its global operations, Toyota could track production and logistics data instantly, respond faster to disruptions and adjust production plans.

Third, digital systems supported sustainability targets. It enables Toyota to optimise resources and logistics to reduce carbon emissions.

### What challenges did Toyota face during the implementation of these frameworks, and how were they mitigated?
Challenges included resistance to change within its workforce and resistance to complying with international regulations such as GDPR.

For employee resistance: COBIT's structured governance helped define clear roles, responsibilities, and the expected benefits of the new digital systems. ITIL's standardised IT service processes supported training and ongoing IT support, helping employees adapt to new digital tools.

To ensure compliance with international regulations, COBIT's built-in control and compliance objectives guided Toyota in building data protection controls into its global IT systems. ITIL maintained stable IT service operations, keeping data-handling processes consistent and auditable.

By using IT governance frameworks, Toyota managed these obstacles and became a more agile organisation.

### How can strategic IT management contribute to sustainability goals in large enterprises?
Strategic IT management aligns IT systems with sustainability objectives.

First, IT systems can collect, analyse, and monitor operational data such as energy consumption, logistics routes and factory output. This data helps optimise resource usage and the logistics chain to lower carbon emissions.

Second, IT governance frameworks like COBIT embed sustainability requirements into IT project evaluation. Management needs to assess environmental impacts alongside cost and risk when approving new IT investments.

Third, digitalisation and automation reduce waste and energy use by replacing inefficient manual processes. Cloud and real-time monitoring platforms allow enterprises to track environmental performance globally.

In summary, strategic IT management embeds sustainability objectives in decision-making and operations to achieve measurable, controllable operational outcomes.

## IT Governance Framework Analysis: ITIL in a Healthcare Context

### ITIL as best-practice guidance for IT service management
ITIL (Information Technology Infrastructure Library) is recognised as a best-practice guidance framework on IT service management (ITSM), providing standardised processes to design, deliver, manage, and continuously improve IT services in an organisation (AXELOS, 2019). Eikebrokk and Iden (2016, p. 14) point out, "the main reasons for adopting ITIL are to become a service-oriented organisation and to deliver IT services that meet business needs". By using ITIL, a new healthcare centre can reduce inefficient operations, improve the patient experience, and perform better risk management.

### Implementing ITIL processes to support clinical staff and patients
Implementing ITIL processes optimises IT operations to better support clinical staff and patients in the health centre. The application of electronic health records (EHR) reduces repetitive manual tasks for doctors and enhances communication among healthcare workers. ITIL-guided workflows ensure this system operates reliably, setting standards like maintaining high availability of the EHR during clinic hours. An online appointment system reduces patient waiting times and promotes safe and efficient care. Overall, these ITIL-driven measures contribute to an improved patient experience.

### Mitigating IT-related risks through ITIL
To mitigate IT-related risks, ITIL requires IT service operations to deliver daily support through incident, problem and service request management to fix IT failures and handle routine user needs. As part of service transition, any EHR software update or system upgrade must be assessed for risks before deployment to avoid disrupting clinical work. Agreed service targets (SLA), established during the service design phase, are implemented to enhance response efficiency, including critical EHR faults being addressed within 30 minutes. ITIL also requires data backup and recovery plans as part of IT service design to prevent data loss.

### IT resource optimisation and sustainability
ITIL can support IT resource optimisation, which contributes to improved operational performance and competitive advantage. ITIL frameworks enable organisations to embed sustainability-focused decisions within IT service design. Digital solutions, such as electronic health records and telehealth platforms, reduce paper waste and travel emissions. Cloud migration and energy-saving hardware can also cut electricity consumption. ITIL helps manage these resources through structured service workflows.

### Benefits, challenges and practical solutions
Adopting ITIL in a health centre delivers multiple benefits. By providing standardised incident management and service request management, ITIL improves IT service quality for clinical staff. ITIL's risk management helps the centre to better handle IT failures and manage cyber threats. However, the centre also faces challenges. The centre must invest in staff training and faces resistance from busy clinical workers. Maintaining ITIL processes relies on sustained resources and cross-department collaboration between IT and medical teams. Poor implementation may also create unnecessary bureaucracy.

Several practical solutions can address the challenges of ITIL adoption within a health centre. To reduce the burden of training, role-specific learning materials can be provided: IT personnel receive comprehensive ITIL training, while clinical staff only learn simplified key operations. To overcome resistance from clinical workers, the centre should communicate how ITIL improves patient-care support and adjust processes based on clinician feedback. To avoid excessive bureaucracy, work should be undertaken according to established priorities, with adequate time allocated for completion over a longer timeframe. Furthermore, appointing a dedicated process owner and holding regular joint meetings between IT and medical departments helps sustain ITIL practices and strengthen cross-team collaboration.
